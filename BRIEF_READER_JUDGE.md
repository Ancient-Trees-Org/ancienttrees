# Reader judge: the brief

The instant run (`.github/workflows/reader-judge.yml`) reads this. It starts minutes after a reader sends a tree or a photograph, so their tree is live while they still remember sending it. Hidde, 2026-11-17: "should be instant honestly". The rules are CLAUDE.md's ("Verify", "Reader input"); this file is only the order of work.

## What you judge

Everything a reader sent that has no verdict yet (and their reports on our trees, see "Reports on our trees" below):

1. `python3 scripts/sightings_inbox.py --status`: photographs of trees we already map (data/sighting-queue.json, files in out/sightings/).
2. Leads in data/leads/_sightings.json with `status: "lead"` and no `hold`: trees we do not map. Their photographs are already on this machine at out/sightings/<sighting id>.jpg (`scripts/fetch_waiting_photos.py` ran before you).

A lead with `status: "lead"` and no `hold` is WAITING, whatever its `why` says. A `why` written before 2026-11-17 ("needs the site named", "no girth", "for a later pass", "a lead") is not a verdict under the closed list; judge it again from the photograph. Do not leave such leads "for the next run": they are the work.

Look at every photograph with Read before you decide. Never judge one blind; if a file is missing, leave that tree for the next run and say so in LOG.md.

## The decision (CLAUDE.md "Verify", the closed list)

A reader's tree goes live when it has a GPS fix or a photograph and is somewhat special. It is held only for a reason from the closed list, written in the lead's `hold` field: `bad_frame`, `wrong_tree`, `wrong_subject`, `not_special`, `dead`, `not_public`, `no_location`, `not_theirs`, and on a real doubt `doubt_location`, `doubt_photo`, `doubt_info`, `inappropriate`. No girth, age, source, site name or "a later pass" is ever a reason. `bad_frame` is narrow (Hidde, 2026-11-18, after looking at seven held frames: "a photo of the trunk is better than nothing", and the Chiang Mai silhouette went live because it had an exact GPS fix): a photo straight up the trunk, or a weak photo of a tree whose GPS fix is at it, goes live; `bad_frame` is only a photo that shows no tree at all to recognise (flowers or leaves alone, a speck in a field) without a GPS fix to find it. A tree held for a reason the reader can fix (`bad_frame`, `no_location`, `doubt_location`, `not_public`, `wrong_tree`, `not_theirs`) gets the standard friendly mail at once: `python3 scripts/hold_reply.py <submission id> <reason> "<tree name>"` (Hidde, 2026-11-18). An app tree gets it too, never "app sighting, so no mail": pass the lead's id (`lead:<sighting id>`) and the script finds the row the app wrote when the tree was sent; a sighting only kept in the reader's own collection has none and gets no mail, which the script says (Hidde, 2026-11-23: "dit moet jij automatisch doen", after two app trees held `not_public` sat unasked). The other reasons get no mail. `dead` holds only a stump, a fallen trunk, or a dead tree with no photograph: a reader's photograph of a standing dead tree that is still striking goes live like any other, the story saying plainly it is dead (Hidde, 2026-11-17, the Ochi chestnut); without a photo the reply asks for one. `not_public` only when the tree cannot be seen from public ground: on private land but visible from a street or path, it goes live with the pin on the viewing spot and an honest `access` line ("seen from the street; the garden is private"); when you cannot tell, hold and the reply asks whether it can be seen from the street. A photograph that shows one of OUR trees (check `nearest_published_m` and the species) belongs on that tree, not on a new page; if two of ours could be it, hold as `wrong_tree` and the reply asks which.

**One route** (Hidde, 2026-11-17): every reader's photograph ends with a verdict as a tree of its own unless it shows one of ours. In the queue (a photo sent for a tree we map) that means: a photograph of ANOTHER tree is the verdict `other_tree`, never `reject` or `hold`; sightings_publish.py turns it into a waiting lead with `sent_for`, and you (or the next run) judge it as a new tree under the closed list. `reject` is only for no tree in frame, a duplicate, or a bad frame. A lead with `unsure_among` was sent with "not sure which tree": it carries no name of ours on purpose; if the photograph shows one of the trees listed, it goes on that tree, otherwise judge it as new. A lead's coordinate from a photo sent for one of ours may be our pin rather than where it was taken (its `why` says so): with no other evidence, hold as `doubt_location` and the reply asks where it stands. **Unless the tree carries its own marker** (Hidde, 2026-11-24, the Šimun Tudor cypress on Marjan, held for this: "this is sort of a special tree no?"): a plaque, a sign or a named plinth beside a path that passes our pin's spot makes it findable, so it goes live with an approximate pin, the marker as its recognition line and the story saying the pin marks the area.

**`not_special` is the rare verdict, not the safe one** (Hidde, 2026-11-24, overruling two holds the same afternoon: "ik vind die palm in florianopolis wel vet waarom niet?", and the golden tulip tree sent for the Solbosch beech, "this also looks like a special tree"). A tree that stands out in its own setting is special: a palm whose crown rises above the forest, a big tree blazing in autumn colour alone on a lawn, a column above the pines. Judge the photograph as a walker would, not against the record books. `not_special` is for an ordinary tree in an ordinary frame (a street tree, a garden shrub, a young planting); when you hesitate, it goes live.

**"Cannot tell it is ours" is rare** (Hidde, 2026-11-25: "is most likely a cedar"). A photograph sent FOR one of our trees, from its square or park, whose shape fits (a conifer's tiered sprays under Christmas lights in Piazza Bra) is that tree: it goes in the strip, never a hold because lights, night or a crowd hide the bark. When an official register entry stands within about 50 m with the same species, the tree is settled: publish on the register's coordinate, girth and height.

**A screenshot waits for its maker's yes** (Hidde, 2026-11-26: "screenshot zijn wel gevaarlijk toch", reversing the line of the day before that let a screenshot through unless it was plainly someone else's). A cropped phone screenshot with no camera data, a social-media crop, letterbox bars or a filter: we cannot tell who took it, and a photo goes up only with its maker's yes. Hold the photograph as `doubt_photo` and ask: `python3 scripts/hold_reply.py <id> doubt_photo "<tree>"`, or `doubt_photo_live` when the tree itself goes live on other sources (a register, our own tree) without the photo (`--again` on a row we already answered). Their yes puts it up the same run; plainly someone else's (a name, likes or buttons over it) stays `not_theirs`.

**A good photo that seems to show another spot than our pin is asked about, never doubted in silence** (Hidde, 2026-11-27, the Genoa cedar: "i dont understan the problem of the cedar the photo looks like a legit phot", then "we should just ask the person if its that tree or another one and ask for location?" and "remember this response for this situation"). A summer photo sent in winter, a photo without camera data or a professional-looking frame is no reason by itself. Hold it as `doubt_location` and ask with the text he approved: `python3 scripts/hold_reply.py <id> photo_elsewhere "<the tree> in <place>" --where "<where our page puts it>" --seen "<what the photo shows>" --kind <species word>`. If they say it is the same tree, the photo goes live. If it is another tree, it becomes a new tree at their spot. A photo only added to the reader's own collection has no submissions row: the script prints the text instead, and a session sends it to the account's address as a one-mail batch.

## Putting it live

- **Every photograph they sent goes up, not only the first** (2026-11-26, a tester: "I uploaded a tree with three photos, the approved version shows only one"; 84 trees showed fewer than were sent). After the trees and photos of this run are live: `python3 scripts/reader_extras.py --sheet out/tmp/extras`, look at every sheet, `--leave <sid> <n> <reason> "what it shows"` only for a reason in its LEAVE_REASONS (duplicate, no_tree, other_tree, person: somebody posing or a selfie, screenshot, not_theirs, withheld), then `--apply`. A weaker frame than the lead is never a reason. Never write a further photograph by hand; preflight refuses a push while one has no verdict, and the congratulation mail tells the reader which stayed off and why.
- **A photo of a tree we map**: verdicts JSON, then `python3 scripts/sightings_publish.py verdicts.json --send` (its docstring has the fields). Never by hand.
- **A new tree**: copy the pattern of `scripts/build_reader_trees_2026_11_17.py` (one script per run, under out/tmp/, never committed): `write_image()` for the photo, `found_by_reader.sighting_id`, `pin_source` from the GPS fix, the reader's own words quoted, their figures stated as theirs, a story of 150 to 250 words, a recognition line, an honest `access` line. Readers' girths stay in the story, never in `girth_cm`. A tree without GPS needs a placeable named site (approximate pin) and at least one `verified_sources` line naming where it stands. data/cities/essaouira.json is a one-tree place. A new place gets intro (60 to 100 words), question_meta, question_answer, question_context (150 to 200 words) and three FAQ.
- **The lead's `location_kind` decides the pin** (Hidde, 2026-11-20, after 31 trees placed by hand had gone out as a confirmed GPS fix): `device` or `photo` is a GPS fix (confirmed, `pin_source` "reader GPS fix"); `placed` (dragged on the map by hand) or empty is approximate, `pin_source` "reader's hand-placed pin", and the story never says "GPS fix" for it. preflight `check_a_hand_placed_pin_is_not_a_fix()`. **No story, FAQ, intro or recognition line says how a pin was obtained**, for any tree (Hidde, 2026-11-20: "wat is met een gps fix is dit voor de gebruiker of voor jou"): write "a reader sent us this tree from …", never "with a GPS fix"; the GPS lives in `pin_source` only. Coordinates a visitor can type in stay welcome. qa `check_no_gps_in_public_copy()`.
- **The lead's `taken_month` goes on the photo** (Hidde, 2026-11-19): sightings_inbox.py writes the photograph's own month on every lead whose date is the photo's (both apps; the website's Add a photo since 2026-11-19; the tip form since 2026-11-20 as `submissions.photo_month`; a tip's send moment is never it); copy it into `photo.taken_month` with `taken_month_basis` "the photograph's own date". Only a lead without one is judged by what it shows, below.
- **A photograph with no date in the file goes into its season by what it shows** (Hidde, 2026-11-17, the Arbour Oak in snow: "als je sneeuw ziet mag die in winter hoor als je twijfelt"): snow or frost means winter, set `photo.taken_month` to 1 (7 south of the equator) with `taken_month_basis` saying why. **Widened 2026-11-19** (Hidde, on a chestnut in copper leaf and cherries in blossom with an empty Seasons row: "waarom staat er geen foto in autumn?", "and here it misses in spring"): autumn colour is autumn (11; 5 south), a spring-flowering tree in blossom is spring (3 to 5, its own flowering month; 9 to 11 south), and when the day it was sent agrees with what it shows, that month. A photo that shows no season (plain green, bare branches alone) simply stays without a month; that is fine (Hidde, 2026-11-20: "het mag zonder maar als de info er is moeten we het opslaan"). What is never fine is a month we know left off: qa `check_a_reader_photo_is_filed_by_season()` fails when the lead carries `taken_month` and the published photo does not.
- Then the lead: `status: "published"`, `published_as`, `judged`, `why` (what the photograph shows). A held one: `status: "held"`, `hold`, `why`. A tree held as `not_theirs` gets the standard friendly mail at once: `python3 scripts/hold_reply.py <submission id> not_theirs "<tree name>"` (Hidde, 2026-11-17: "doe zelf een foto maken, maar dan aardig").
- And the same sighting in data/sightings-processed.json: `done[<sighting id>].outcome` becomes `published` or `held`. Left at `lead`, the next intake matches the photograph to the tree you just made and queues it again.
- `python3 scripts/judgement.py --scan`, then each verdict in data/judgements.json, and a `sight:<sighting id>` entry for every contributor photograph now on a page.

## Reports on our trees

Since 2026-11-17 the same run takes every report a reader sends about a tree we already map (Hidde: picked up at once, like the readers' trees). `python3 scripts/tree_reports.py --open` prints them with their words; the intake already closed the moved-pin nudges of a few metres on a confirmed pin (`--auto`). Close each one you handle with `python3 scripts/tree_reports.py --thank pin|dead ...` (the only mails a run sends on a report) or `--close <id> holds` (see "Who answers"). A report you cannot settle in this run stays open: say why in LOG.md, never close it to empty the list.

**A photograph's GPS beats a pin moved by hand** (Hidde, 2026-11-17, on York, Brighton and Fairchild: "vol GPS over het algemeen, tenzij je op de foto ook kunt zien dat het echt ver weg is"): when a reader's hand-moved pin disagrees with a GPS fix from a photograph of the tree (theirs or another reader's), the GPS fix stays, unless the photograph itself plainly shows it was taken from far off (the whole tree small in a wide view); a pin moved first and photographed after is fine too. Keep the pin, send the pin thank-you, decide it yourself: this never goes to Hidde.

**When we cannot tell who is right, the reader wins** (Hidde, 2026-11-17: "als we zelf niet weten hoe het zit kunnen we de gebruiker toch of zijn suggestie volgen of vragen of hij ergens een bron heeft"): a pin goes where they put it (`location_precision` stays honest: a hand-placed spot is `approximate` unless their GPS fix stands at it), and a fact we cannot settle is answered by asking, kindly, whether they know a source, never by defending ours.

By reason (CLAUDE.md's rules, in short):

- **dead / gone**: checked and removed the same day when it holds (a news item, a council notice, a second report, a reader's photograph of the stump): first `python3 scripts/tree_reports.py --thank dead <report id> <tree id>` (the standard thank-you, no link since the page goes; it reads the name before the tree leaves data/cities), then `python3 scripts/retire.py <city> <id> --reason "..."`. No evidence either way: leave the tree and close `holds`; a draft for Hidde asks for a photograph.
- **wrong location** and **moved pin**: the reader's place replaces an approximate pin when their words, a grid reference, a GPS fix or a photograph make it a real spot (convert a grid reference yourself); record `pin_source` ("reader report <id>, <what they gave>") and keep `location_precision` honest: `confirmed` only for a GPS fix at the tree. Every pin report on a tree of ours closes with `python3 scripts/tree_reports.py --thank pin <report id> <tree id>`, moved or kept (Hidde, 2026-11-17: "beide zijn toch een bedankje waard"; the text claims no move), unless they wrote words of their own (see "Who answers" below). A moved pin on the reader's own new tree, made while adding it, is part of that tree: no mail, the congratulation comes when it goes live. A confirmed pin moves only on evidence that ours is wrong (their photo, a source, a move of hundreds of metres onto a site the story names). A tree the same account added itself: take their move. A move that lands on another of our trees or in open country without a reason: `holds`.
- **could not tell which tree**: a recognition line (`how_to_recognise`, BRIEF_WRITING.md's rule: restated from what the entry holds) or a trunk photograph. When the row came from the app's collect flow (a sighting id in `tree`, a list of candidates in the words) it is the reader asking which tree their photograph shows: settle it with the sighting (sightings_inbox.py) and close.
- **on the map twice**: same trunk by pins, photos and sources, then keep the richer entry and `retire.py --reason "duplicate of <id>"`. Never on the reader's word alone.
- **something else** / **correction**: a fact they correct is checked like any fact (a source, or their own photograph or sign) and fixed in every language the story exists in; an age they give goes on the page "by a reader's estimate".

**Who answers** (Hidde, 2026-11-17: general feedback "is niet dood of verplaatsen pin maar als er complexe input binnen komt, geschreven tekst"): the run mails only the two standard thank-yous (`--thank pin`, `--thank dead`), and only when the report carries no words of their own beyond the chip, a coordinate or a grid reference. Anything a reader wrote themselves (a question, a story, praise, a correction in sentences, a product question like Android) is general feedback and his to read: act on what it says (fix the fact, move the pin), never write a `reply_text`, put a suggested answer in drafts/reply-reader-<report id>-<date>.md when one would help, and close the report `holds` with no reply. If you fixed the fact the standard mail fits, but they also wrote words: no standard mail either, the draft carries it. A report with nothing you can act on and no draft is closed `holds`. The digest shows him their words whole.
- **collected far**: the photograph decides, through the sighting queue; close the report once it is judged.

## Tips without a photograph

`python3 scripts/judgement.py --scan` also stubs every tree or place a reader sent as words alone (`tip:<submission id>`, kind "a tip without a photograph"); `--unjudged` lists every stub still without a verdict. Read the tip in full (`python3 scripts/inbox.py` or the submissions row), then: a tree we already map gets `publish` with the tree named (nothing to add, or their words as a note); a tree a source confirms (two sources, or one register plus a life check, per CLAUDE.md "Verify") and that can be placed honestly ships as a reader-found tree (`found_by_reader.tip`, their words quoted, no photograph needed, a recognition line instead); otherwise it is recorded as a lead in data/leads/<place>.json with what is missing, verdict `lead`, and, when one fact would settle it (where exactly, is it public), a reply asking for it (`tree_reports.py`-style reply_text on the submission, or a draft when their own words call for one). Write every verdict with `python3 scripts/judgement.py --verdict tip:<id> publish|lead|hold "why"`.

## General feedback

`python3 scripts/feedback.py --open` prints every feedback message without a verdict (one-tap votes and reports are left out). Handle the easy ones and record each with `python3 scripts/feedback.py --close <id> handled|needs_hidde|doubt "what you did"` in one line of your own words, never theirs and never a name (the ledger is public). **Something broken is a bug, never his call** (2026-11-24, report 1491: "the country does not stay" sat two days as `needs_hidde`): whenever a reader says the app or the site does not work as it should, in feedback, a mail reply or a note, find its BUG row in data/code-audit.json or add one (`python3 scripts/escaped.py --add APP|WEB "<what>" --file <path> --found-by reader`), then `python3 scripts/feedback.py --close <id> bug <bug-id> "what you did"`. The row is rung-2 work until a test closes it. Give the row what will work once it is fixed, in a reader's plain words (`python3 scripts/escaped.py --reader-line <bug-id> "the country you choose in Edit profile now stays"`, plus `--reader-line-nl` when they wrote in Dutch): the reader is mailed that line, never their own words (scripts/bug_fixed_mail.py, Hidde 2026-11-25): for an app fix first the day it is on main ("comes with the next app update"), then when the build is in their store (Hidde, 2026-11-26: "onthou die volgorde"); a website fix once, when it is live. A session that uploads an Android build to Play adds its versionCode to data/android-releases.json. A reply for the reader may still go to drafts/ for his yes; the bug does not wait for it. Handled: a bug you fixed, a tree tip turned into a lead or a tree, an idea filed in PRODUCT_TODO.md, a question the product already answers, thanks that need nothing. Never send or queue a reply to feedback (Hidde, 2026-11-17: "lijkt me beter als ik dat zelf lees"): when an answer would help, write it to drafts/reply-reader-<id>-<date>.md and close the row `needs_hidde`. A decision outside the four verbs, or anything you cannot settle, is `needs_hidde` or `doubt` with the reason. Those reach him in the digest's "Open for you"; nothing else does.

## Mail replies

A reader's mail reply to one of our reader mails arrives as a feedback row with `page` "mail-reply:<id>" (the id of that reader's newest row), their words in `why` (scripts/reader_mail_replies.py, since 2026-11-18). First check whether it is already handled: its facts already on the tree's page, or LOG.md says a session answered it; then close it `handled` and do nothing else. Otherwise act on it as their answer: a place, a "you can see it from the lane", which tree it is, or a photo they mention re-opens their held lead (status `lead`, no `hold`, the new fact in `why`) and you judge it again this run; an age, a girth or a story for a live tree goes on its page as theirs ("by a reader's estimate"), never quoted word for word without their yes (CLAUDE.md "Words that came by mail"). **A yes to a question we asked is acted on at once, never `needs_hidde`** (Hidde, 2026-11-20, on Jairam's "Of course :)" to our quote ask: "dont wait for my ok if a user agrees to our question"): a yes to "may we quote you" sets `quote_ok` on their row in data/outreach-words.json (their words and the date; `private_store.py pull` first, `push` after) and the quote goes on the page this run; a yes to the Android test adds them as a tester (`android_testers.py --add`); a wish to be ambassador of a place, in any words, is the request and the yes (Hidde, same day, on Teresa in La Tène: "why do i need to agree on ambassador ship"): `ambassador.py --grant <account> <place> --send` when the row has an account (it sends the standard "Looking after <place>" mail once per person, Hidde 2026-11-22: a yes by mail got the badge and no mail), else `--grant-named`, then on with the rest. Then `feedback.py --close <id> handled "what you did"`. Something that does not work is a bug (see "General feedback": `escaped.py --add`, then `--close <id> bug <bug-id>`). Anything else they wrote (a question, praise, a product idea) is general feedback: `needs_hidde`, and a suggested answer in drafts/ when one helps. Never mail them from this run.

## Readers' words

Then `python3 scripts/reader_words.py`: each reader's note on a live tree whose words no story quotes yet. Put the usable ones on the page (CLAUDE.md "Readers' words go on the page, quoted"): unnamed, their facts stated as theirs, an age they give as "by a reader's estimate", in every language the story exists in. Not usable, and closed with `--done <row id> "why"`: text pasted from a chatbot or a website (headings like "Key Details", bullet summaries; check its facts, never quote it as theirs), an instruction to us, or thanks. A fact from it that a source confirms may go in the story as a fact.

## Before you push

`python3 scripts/qa.py --source-only` must say nothing to fix: it refuses a reader's raw account id in any public file (write `people.ref(uid)`, never the id), which on 2026-11-17 slipped past a run's push and failed every deploy for over an hour.


`python3 scripts/preflight.py` must print 0 problems and `python3 scripts/i18ncheck.py` must be clean: a new tree in a place with a kept translation needs that overlay's tree entry, and every count in the place's texts and overlays follows the new number. Then one LOG.md entry ("## <date> reader judge: ..."), newest first, commit, `git pull --rebase --autostash`, push.

## Limits

About 20 minutes. Readers' trees and reports only: no research passes, no outreach, no enrichment. If more trees wait than fit, do the newest first (they are the ones whose senders are waiting) and leave the rest for the next run; the workflow starts again on the next tree that arrives.
